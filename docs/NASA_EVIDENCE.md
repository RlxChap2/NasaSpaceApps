# NASA evidence

The [challenge page](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/health-monitoring-software/) asks for a visual tool that gathers health indicators and helps a crew understand their status. The Global Organizing Team confirmed that meaningfully using a research resource listed on this challenge's page can satisfy the NASA source requirement. The project uses HRP research to explain what the prototype tracks and where a separate instrument or dedicated assessment would be needed. [GO Team clarification](https://github.com/nasa/spaceapps/discussions/1994).

## Rule and dataset claims

- [NASA-STD-3001 Volume 1 Rev C](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/nasa-std-3001-vol-1-rev-c-with-signature.pdf) appears in the catalog and source map. NASA lists Volume 1 under crew health and Volume 2 under human factors, habitability and environmental health. Exact requirement IDs and clause-to-rule derivations are missing. The audit replaced the check-in and research copy that described the rules as a NASA engine or called the examples calibrated. [NASA standards index](https://standards.nasa.gov/human-factors-and-health).
- The [Inspiration4 civilian crew study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11357997/) describes observations of a short mission and 11-point alertness and mood survey ratings. It provides research context. The workbook's source text also cites a [sleep and circadian review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12155433/). Neither reference, as mapped in this repo, establishes the prototype's cutoffs, fatigue-to-stress conversion or proposed actions for longer missions.
- The [OSDR file map](../api/data/input/osdr_files_map.csv) links [OSD-569](https://osdr.nasa.gov/bio/repo/data/studies/OSD-569/), [OSD-575](https://osdr.nasa.gov/bio/repo/data/studies/OSD-575/) and [OSD-656](https://osdr.nasa.gov/bio/repo/data/studies/OSD-656). OSD-656 and OSD-575 list CC0-1.0 on the study pages. The catalog also references [PhysioNet MMASH](https://physionet.org/content/mmash/) as a terrestrial wearable study. The app uses catalog metadata, with zero parsed lab rows in the audited database. The 40 daily demo records are synthetic.
- The [reference CSV](../api/data/input/reference_thresholds.csv) has 13 rows. [engine.py](../api/app/engine.py) contains nine hard-coded numerical rule predicates. The CSV supplies displayed names, ranges, action text and links. HRV, cabin temperature, cabin noise and a dedicated fatigue rule have catalog rows only. Some rule boundaries and nominal ranges disagree for inputs allowed by the API. Changing display text in the CSV cannot change the executable comparisons.
- The rule output is a demonstration of transparent logic. Evidence for clinical accuracy, NASA clearance, verified calibration or use in flight was not found. Use fictional readings and describe any displayed suggestions as examples for review. Use approved mission procedures and qualified medical support in actual operations.
